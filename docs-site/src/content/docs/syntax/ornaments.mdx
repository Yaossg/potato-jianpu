## Fermatas

Use `(.)` after a note-like item to render a fermata above it. The suffix comes
before any duration marks and may attach to a pitch note, whole chord, rest,
drum note, or tied note.

<JianpuSource
  title="Fermata source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(.)- {1 3 5}(.) 2(.) | 0(.) X(.) 2 ~2(.)`}
/>

===

/>

## Mordents

Potato Jianpu currently supports upper and lower mordents. Write the ornament immediately after a pitch note or closing chord brace, before any duration suffix.

Use `(^)` for an upper mordent and `(v)` for a lower mordent.
The upper-mordent glyph is plain, while the lower-mordent glyph has a vertical stroke.

<JianpuSource
  title="Mordent source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(^) 2(v) 3 - | 5(^)/ 5(v)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

<ScorePreview
  title="Rendered mordents"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(^) 2(v) 3 - | 5(^)/ 5(v)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

The suffix comes before dashes, slashes, dots, or other duration marks.

<JianpuSource title="Mordents before duration suffixes" source={`1(^)- 1(v)/ 2(^).`} />

Mordents attach to pitch notes and whole chords, but not to rests, drum notes, or tied notes. On a chord, the glyph appears above the whole chord and the highest sounding member receives the mordent while the other members sustain.

For example, `{1 3 5}(^)` ornaments `5` in C major and keeps `1` and `3` sounding.

## Turns

Turns use the same post-note suffix position. Use `(z)` for a normal turn and `(s)` for an inverted turn.

<JianpuSource
  title="Turn source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(z) 2(s) 3 - | 5(z)/ 5(s)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

<ScorePreview
  title="Rendered turns"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(z) 2(s) 3 - | 5(z)/ 5(s)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

Like mordents, turns attach to pitch notes or whole chords and come before any duration suffix. A chord turn changes its highest sounding member while the other members sustain.

## Trills

Use `(tr)` for a trill. Use `(tr~)` for a long trill with a wavy extension line. Both forms follow the anchor pitch and come before any duration suffix.

<JianpuSource
  title="Trill source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(tr) 2(tr~)- 0 | 5(tr)/ 5(tr)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

<ScorePreview
  title="Rendered trills"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(tr) 2(tr~)- 0 | 5(tr)/ 5(tr)/ 1' - 0`}
/>

The long-trill line is drawn only when its anchor has at least one duration dash. It covers only that anchor's written duration, ends before the next onset or current measure barline, and does not span separate notes or tied-note groups. Without a duration dash, the renderer keeps the trill glyph, omits the extension line, and reports a warning.

Trills attach to pitch notes or whole chords. The upper auxiliary pitch follows the current key signature and any active accidental for that scale degree in the measure. A chord trill alternates its highest sounding member while the other members sustain without repeated attacks.

## Slides

Use `(~)` after a source pitch to slide into the immediately following pitch note in the same track. The suffix comes before any duration marks on the source note.

<JianpuSource
  title="Slide source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(~)- 5 3 | 5(~)- 1 0`}
/>

<ScorePreview
  title="Rendered slides"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(~)- 5 3 | 5(~)- 1 0`}
/>

The renderer infers the direction from the two pitches. Slides may cross a measure boundary or a source line. Both source and target must be ordinary single pitch notes. A rest, drum note, chord, tied target, or missing target is invalid. When both notes have the same sounding pitch, the slide is ignored and produces a warning.

## Grace Notes

Grace notes are also written as post-note ornament suffixes. The suffix always attaches to the pitch note on its left. Use `(<...)` for grace notes rendered and played before the anchor note, and `(>...)` for grace notes rendered and played after it.

<JianpuSource
  title="Grace note source"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(<2) 2(>3) 3(<45) 5(>6')`}
/>

<ScorePreview
  title="Rendered grace notes"
  source={`time: 4/4
===
1(<2) 2(>3) 3(<45) 5(>6')`}
/>

Inside the grace-note suffix, write 1, 2, 4, or another power-of-two number of pitch notes using the normal accidental and octave syntax. A single grace note renders as an eighth note with one underline, two grace notes render as sixteenth notes with two underlines, four grace notes render with three underlines, and so on. Do not write rests, drum notes, chords, ties, dynamics, or duration marks inside the suffix.

Grace notes attach to pitch notes or whole chords. On a chord, the grace sequence modifies the onset of the whole chord rather than one member: it reserves one quarter of the chord's written duration, so every chord member is shortened equally. Before-grace notes lead into that shortened complete chord; after-grace notes begin after it. Chords are not accepted inside the grace-note suffix itself.

## Playback And Export

The playback behavior below also applies to MIDI, WAV, and MP3 export.

Fermatas extend their attached item's written duration by a factor of 1.5.

Each mordent expands into a short main-neighbor-main figure, followed by the remaining duration of the written note.

The upper mordent uses the next scale degree. The lower mordent uses the previous scale degree. The neighbor pitch follows the current key signature and any active accidental for that scale degree in the measure.

Each turn expands into a short four-note figure, followed by the remaining duration of the written note. A normal turn plays upper-anchor-lower-anchor. An inverted turn plays lower-anchor-upper-anchor.

A trill expands across the full written duration. It starts on the anchor, alternates with the upper auxiliary pitch at thirty-second-note density, and always keeps at least one complete anchor-upper pair. The `(tr)` and `(tr~)` forms produce identical playback events.

A slide uses the final quarter of its source note's written duration for equal chromatic semitone steps through the target pitch. The following target note then keeps its own normal attack and duration.

Grace notes expand inside the written duration of the anchor note. Before-grace notes sound first, followed by the shortened anchor note. After-grace notes use the end of the anchor duration. Multiple grace notes split the grace duration evenly.

Chord ornaments use two distinct playback rules. Mordents, turns, and trills ornament only the highest sounding member after resolving the key, accidentals, and octave marks; the other members remain held across the expanded events instead of receiving repeated attacks. Grace notes affect the whole chord onset, so all members share the shortened chord duration before or after the grace sequence.

SVG, PNG, and PDF export render fermatas, mordents, turns, and trills above the attached item using the bundled SMuFL ornament font. A `(tr~)` long trill adds a wavy path through the anchor note's written duration. Slides render in the source note's upper-right area as fixed-radius rising or falling quarter-circle arcs with an arrowhead. The target pitch determines the direction but its position does not stretch the mark. Grace notes render as small Jianpu figures beside the anchor note: before-grace notes on the left, after-grace notes on the right. Each grace figure has one or more short underlines whose lower edge connects to the anchor note with a quarter-circle arc. Downloaded SVG and PDF output embed the font needed for SMuFL ornament symbols, while PNG export loads or embeds the same font before rasterization.
